How to Sort Laundry Like an Industry Pro

By Priya Sharma • March 18, 2026 • 6 min read

Sorting laundry properly is the single most important step in garment preservation. While many people toss everything together in a rush, professional textile specialists know that correct sorting prevents color bleeding, fabric friction damage, and premature wear. Master these industry sorting strategies to keep your wardrobe looking immaculate.

1. Separate by Color Intensity, Not Just Whites and Darks

Beyond separating whites from darks, professional processing divides loads into four distinct color tiers: pure whites, lights (pastels, light grays, light pinks), brights/mediums (reds, oranges, royal blues), and darks (black, navy, deep brown). This prevents dye transfer from newer garments onto lighter fabrics.

2. Sort by Fabric Weight and Texture

Heavy items like denim jeans, cotton hoodies, and bath towels create aggressive friction inside the washing machine drum. Mixing them with delicate silk blouses, activewear mesh, or fine cotton tees causes pilling, stretching, and fiber abrasion. Always wash heavy fabrics separately from lightweight garments.

"Sorting by fabric weight is the secret weapon commercial cleaners use to extend the lifespan of high-end garments."

3. Check Lint Contributors vs. Lint Collectors

Certain fabrics shed microscopic fibers during agitation (like fleece sweaters, cotton flannel, and chenille towels). Other items act as magnets for lint (like dark corduroy, microfiber cloths, and spandex activewear). Washing lint-givers with lint-collectors results in frustrating cleaning sessions later—keep them separate!

4. Tailor Your Water Temperature by Category

Commercial laundering utilizes specific thermal dynamics to lift oils and bacteria safely:

  • Cold Water (20°C–30°C): Ideal for darks, bright colors, activewear, and delicate items to prevent shrinking and fading.
  • Warm Water (40°C): Perfect for synthetic blends, cotton garments with moderate wear, and everyday mixed loads.
  • Hot Water (60°C+): Reserved exclusively for heavily soiled whites, bed linens, towels, and sanitation cycles.

5. Inspect Pockets, Zippers, and Fasteners

Before throwing sorted items into their respective hampers, ensure all zippers are zipped up (to prevent metal teeth from snagging other clothes), buttons are unbuttoned (to avoid stress on threads), and pockets are completely empty to prevent ink stains or paper tissue explosions.
